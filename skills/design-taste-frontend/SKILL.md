Selective guidance from Leonxlnx/taste-skill, not the upstream executable toolkit. [Source pin and modifications](../gold-web-ui/upstream-manifest.json); [license](LICENSE).

Use [gold-web-ui](../gold-web-ui/SKILL.md) and its foundations/verification. This is an alternative **single design lead**, not another set of absolute rules layered on Frontend Design. The approved brief, existing brand/system, accessibility, content and GOLD gates win. Review does not authorize fixes; polish does not authorize redesign. No Stop hooks, finish-gate scripts, asset downloaders, browser checkers, upstream CSS/HTML kits or project-state writers are bundled. Never bypass browser/tool permissions.

## Site-level direction
1. Identify the company/product, real offer, audience, primary action and evidence. Write plainly about what is sold and why it matters; let the brand have its own voice. Do not invent facts, customer logos, testimonials, metrics, hours or prices.
2. Establish one coherent system: palette suited to the subject, type hierarchy, shared content edges, spacing and reusable interaction patterns. Inspect existing design tokens/components before proposing replacements.
3. Plan a clear progression. Each section has one main idea and a useful visual/content role: product, use, parts, context, comparison or typography. Vary presentation to support the story instead of cloning the same three-card layout.
4. Show the real thing: actual product UI or appropriate licensed/client imagery. Use useful diagrams when explanation needs them. Verify asset rights and attribution; external images/fonts/icons are not included or licensed merely by this skill. Do not fetch or generate assets without task scope.
5. Choose one memorable visual moment if it supports the brief. Keep surrounding content calm and useful. Shader/3D work is optional, expensive and subject-specific; do not add it merely to signal sophistication.

## Adapt, do not obey house rules blindly
Upstream's screen-height sections, compulsory hero entrances, hover micro-animations and no-caption rule are **not universal requirements** here. Content, reflow/zoom, small screens, reduced motion and accessibility take precedence. Use captions where they communicate meaning or fulfill legal/accessibility needs. Do not force sections to fill a viewport or prevent content from extending naturally.

Motion should clarify state, preserve keyboard/touch operation, be interruptible and have a reduced alternative. CSS first; no mandatory animation library. Equal visual edges and consistent icon treatment are good defaults, not reasons to break an existing system. Never replace a real logo with an invented imitation or omit required attribution.

## Review the complete site
Use actual desktop/intermediate/mobile renders when available, then test the primary path, content extremes, focus, zoom/reflow and reduced motion. Check that every meaningful visual loads, copy remains specific, and sections form a coherent narrative. Source review cannot claim a browser finish gate passed. Report the capability limits and distinguish style suggestions from confirmed defects; keep repairs inside approved scope.
